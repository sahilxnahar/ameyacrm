# Audit before go-live — what was found, and what it cost

You asked for a green signal. **This was not a green audit.** Two of the
findings were already costing you production, and one of them was silently
corrupting the books. All are fixed and verified; the detail is below so you can
judge the work rather than take my word for it.

---

## The one that locked you out

**Every signed-in screen was failing, not just the login.** The error you
photographed is the *root* error boundary — the one that only renders when the
signed-in **layout** throws. The layout read four things from the database on
every route with no fallback, so a single missing column took down all ~200
screens at once.

I reproduced it exactly: drop `User.topNavPrefs` and every page returns that
screen, with `P2022 The column User.topNavPrefs does not exist`.

Why it stayed broken:

- The **Repair button** that fixes database drift lives *inside* the layout that
  fails. The recovery tool sat behind the failure it recovers from.
- Even if you had reached it, it would not have worked. `init-schema-sql.ts` —
  the SQL both `/api/setup` and Repair execute — had drifted **four releases
  behind**: 14 tables and 25 columns short, `User.topNavPrefs` among them. A
  fresh install was broken from first login too.
- And it only ever emitted `CREATE TABLE`. On a database where the table already
  exists, that fails harmlessly and **no missing column is ever added** — which
  is precisely the shape of your outage.

Fixed:

- The layout degrades instead of dying: default navigation, no project switcher,
  and a red banner naming what is missing.
- `init-schema-sql.ts` is regenerated by `scripts/build-init-schema.mjs` from the
  live Prisma schema, and now emits `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` **plus**
  `ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS` for all 2,663 columns, guarded enums,
  indexes and foreign keys. Verified: a database built from the *old* artifact —
  39 things missing, with real user rows in it — repairs to **zero drift, zero
  errors, data intact**.
- `POST /api/admin/repair` runs it without rendering the app, because `/api/*`
  sits outside the broken layout.
- The four layout-critical columns are now first in the drift checker, so the
  banner names the fatal ones before anything else.

## The one that was also stopping logins

**Password reset and invitations were dead.** `middleware.ts` did not list
`/forgot-password`, `/reset-password` or `/set-password`, so every reset email
and every invite link redirected to `/login` — the one screen that person cannot
get past. No user could reset a password and **no invited user could ever
accept**. It failed silently, because being bounced to a login page looks like
the system working. The buyer, channel-partner and vendor portals were in the
same state.

While fixing it I found the opposite error in the same list: matching was
`startsWith`, so `/pay` also matched `/payments` and quietly took the signed-in
payments screen out of the gate. Matching is now whole-segment, and
`tests/public-paths.test.ts` fails if either mistake returns.

## The one that was corrupting the books

**A reversal moved the trial balance by twice the amount, and reported itself as
balanced.** `reverse()` marks the original REVERSED and posts the opposite
entry; every reader filtered `status: 'POSTED'`, which **drops the original and
keeps the reversal**.

Measured, not theorised — cancel a ₹10,00,000 payment:

```
bank moved by  +10,00,000   (want 0)
cost moved by  -10,00,000   (want 0)
trial balance says balanced: true
```

Bank you do not have, negative expenses, and no warning — because both sides
moved together. It hit the trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, every party ledger
and budget-vs-actual. A cancel-then-restore left the payment in the cash book and
entirely absent from the books.

Fixed with one shared, documented predicate (`COUNTS_TOWARDS_BALANCE`) used by
every reader, and `tests/reversal-arithmetic.test.ts` now proves a reversal nets
to zero in the trial balance, in a party ledger, across cancel-then-restore, and
in the Tally mirror.

## Also fixed

**Money**

- A payment awaiting approval was counted as **spent** in six places — the spend
  report, vendor ledger totals, cost-to-complete, the TDS dashboard, material
  insights, and the vendor's own portal, which told the vendor they had been
  paid. The cash book listed it in the Out column while its totals excluded it,
  so the rows did not add up to the stated closing balance.
- `Voucher.amount` meant the bill on one screen and the cheque on another. TDS
  and retention are held back from the bill, so the cash book overstated cash out
  on every such payment and the ledger reconstructed a cost that was too high by
  the same amount. It now stores what actually leaves the bank, and the form says
  so.
- An RA bill's ad-hoc **deduction** (usually a mobilisation advance being
  recovered) vanished from the ledger: project cost understated by the recovery,
  and the advance stranded on the balance sheet as an asset for ever. It now
  clears `1140 Advances to vendors`.
- Account `2155 Labour cess payable (BOCW)` existed in code and in no database —
  seeding only runs on an empty chart — so every contractor payment with cess
  would have saved and silently never posted. The migration adds it.
- The **MSME 45-day clock** was never stopped. A bill paid on day 10 still turned
  OVERDUE on day 46 and stayed there, so the tracker's exposure permanently
  included paid bills and every future settlement carried a s.43B(h) warning that
  was not true. It now closes on payment and re-opens if that payment is
  withdrawn.
- Cancelling or deleting a payment did not reverse its ledger entry, and reported
  a failed reversal identically to "nothing to reverse". Hard delete would have
  orphaned a live journal entry; it now refuses.
- The invoice counter seed in the migration matched nothing — invoice numbers are
  `INV-2026-0157`, not `INV-157` — so it seeded to 0 and the next invoice would
  have collided with an existing number on every save until it caught up.
  Purchase orders, partner codes and payment requests were still using
  `count()+1`, which reissues a number the moment one is deleted; all four are
  now atomic and seeded from what exists.
- The migration is now one transaction, with `lock_timeout`, instead of four
  independent steps that could half-apply.

**Security**

- The device-approval code — a full authentication factor — used `Math.random()`
  while the sibling sign-in code deliberately used a CSPRNG. Now both do.
- `connectorInstalls()` and `installedPackages()` were exported server actions
  with **no permission check at all**; the first returned every connector's raw
  config. Every exported function in a `'use server'` file is a POST endpoint
  whether or not a page imports it.
- An Admin could grant the Admin role every permission in the system, walking
  through the maker-checker separations the code is careful about elsewhere.
  You can no longer rewrite the grants of the role you hold.
- The payment approval **threshold** was guarded by the same permission that
  raises payments, so the payer could set it to zero. It now needs
  `admin.setting.manage`.
- With no mail provider configured, `sendEmail` logged to the console and
  returned success — so a device-approval code the user never received was
  reported as sent. `delivered` is now distinct from `ok`, and the screen says
  the code is in the server log.

**Interface and speed**

- The nine new registers built a malformed URL when you filtered by project
  (`?view=contracts?project=…`), which dropped you back on the first tab with no
  filter applied.
- One shared spinner state turned *every* Issue, Pay, Approve and Reject button
  on screen into a spinner when one was clicked. Now per-row.
- `window.prompt` for a rejection reason — blocked in sandboxed frames and by
  Chrome's "prevent additional dialogs", and it returns nothing silently when
  suppressed. Replaced with real dialogs. **Pay** on a vendor bill fired
  immediately on one click with no amount shown; it now confirms.
- 1- and 2-tile registers were forced into a 3-column grid, leaving a lone tile a
  third of the width — the 13-inch alignment complaint. Register tables had an
  overflow wrapper that did nothing without a min-width.
- Unit tiles used dark text with no dark-mode variant, so the whole inventory
  matrix was unreadable in dark mode.
- The site-log date was computed during render, so between 00:00 and 05:30 IST
  the server and phone disagreed about the day.
- The unposted-payments banner loaded **every** journal entry on every ledger
  page view and could never see a voucher older than the most recent 2,000 — one
  anti-join now, and only for people who can act on it. The Tally mirror made
  1–2 queries *per line* on the user's own request (~25,000 for a 2,000-row
  import) — now one round trip per entry. The tabbed registers fetched every
  tab's full rows to render one; they now count the others.

## Deliberately not changed

**App Exchange** still lists 148 integrations with four real drivers. Excluded at
your instruction. Do not demo that screen.

**Certified-but-unpaid RA bills are still off the books.** Vendor bills are
accrual (cost booked on receipt); RA bills only post at settlement. The two
halves of your cost base are on different bases, so month-end accrued liability
is understated. That is a decision about how you want the books to work, not a
bug, and I did not want to make it for you.

**Inter-state (IGST) purchases** post as CGST+SGST. Account `1153 Input IGST`
exists and nothing writes to it, and `createVendorBill` has no place-of-supply
field, so this needs a small design decision before it can be fixed properly.
Output GST on invoices is driven by a checkbox rather than the buyer's GSTIN,
which the GSTR-1 builder derives independently — those two can disagree.

**Opening balances** ignore the project filter, ignore `openingAsOf`, and have no
contra entry, so entering cash before capital reports the books as out of
balance. It bites on migration day, not before.

## Verification

- `tsc --noEmit` clean. `next build` clean, 59 static pages.
- **566 tests pass**, run twice against a live PostgreSQL to prove they are
  re-runnable, and once against a database built from the migration.
- The reversal arithmetic, the approval exclusions, the MSME clock, the
  settlement rules and the drift repair are each covered by tests that failed
  before the fix and pass after it.
- `DB-DRIFT-CHECK.sql` returns zero rows against a database built from the new
  init schema, and correctly names what is missing when columns are removed.
